Classic Literature

Privacy

Classic Literature needs nothing about you: no account, no name, no email.

The connector

A title or name you ask about is matched against the corpus and then dropped. It is not stored and not logged. For each call we record one event in PostHog: which tool ran, whether it worked, and how long it took. That event carries nothing that identifies you or your conversation: no IP address, no account, and none of your words.

The server runs on Vercel, which keeps standard request logs (time, path, status) under its own privacy policy. The request body, which holds your question, is not written to those logs.

This website

We count visits with Vercel Web Analytics and PostHog: the pages viewed, and whether a visit came from the connector (links it hands out carry src=mcp). Links carry the page and the book or author you were looking at, never anything about you.

The data

Measurements of public-domain texts from Project Gutenberg and published research. Cormac McCarthy's novels are in copyright and are not redistributed; only derived measurements appear.

Questions: open an issue at github.com/2016judea/classic-literature/issues.

Every number comes from measurements of the books' full texts, published with the code at github.com/2016judea/classic-literature.
